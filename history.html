History of Odisha

Odisha's history spans thousands of years, beginning with prehistoric settlements and ancient maritime commerce. Known formerly as Kalinga, the region is famous for the 261 BCE Kalinga War, which transformed Emperor Ashoka, and later became a center for art, temple architecture (including Konark and Puri), and Odia culture, finally becoming the first linguistic state in India on April 1, 1936.

Ancient Period (Pre-6th Century CE)

Known as Kalinga (or Utkala/Odra), the region was a maritime power trading with Southeast Asia. The 261 BCE Kalinga War was a pivotal moment, causing massive loss of life, which led Ashoka to convert to Buddhism, making the region a major center for Buddhist learning (Dhauli, Ratnagiri).

Powerful dynasties, including the Sailodbhavas, Bhauma-Karas, and Somavamsis, ruled, constructing significant temples such as the Lingaraj Temple.

Classical Period & Regional Dynasties (6th–16th Century):

Powerful dynasties, including the Sailodbhavas, Bhauma-Karas, and Somavamsis, ruled, constructing significant temples such as the Lingaraj Temple.

Kalinga War (261 BCE):

The Mauryan Emperor Ashoka invaded Kalinga. The immense bloodshed caused him to renounce violence and embrace Buddhism.

Kharavela Dynasty: In the 1st century BCE, King Kharavela of the Mahameghavahana dynasty established a powerful empire, expanding its influence across India.

Medieval Period (7th - 16th Century CE)

Dynastic Rule: Several dynasties flourished, including the Sailodbhavas, Bhauma-Karas, and Somavamsis.

Temple Architecture: The Somavamsi dynasty built the Lingaraj Temple in Bhubaneswar, while the Eastern Ganga dynasty later constructed the iconic Konark Sun Temple and Jagannath Temple in Puri.

Foreign Rule and Colonial Era (1568 - 1947)

Afghan & Mughal Rule: In 1568, the region was conquered by the Sultanate of Bengal, leading to the loss of its political independence. Later, it passed to the Mughal Empire by 1593.

Maratha Administration: From 1751, the Marathas ruled, during which period literature and poetry flourished.

British Occupation (1803-1947): The British East India Company took over, dividing the region into different administrative provinces.

Modern Odisha (1936 - Present)

Formation of Province (1936): On April 1, 1936, the province of Odisha was formed on a linguistic basis (Odia-speaking people), a day celebrated as Utkala Divas.

Post-Independence: After India's independence in 1947, princely states merged with the state. In 1948, the capital shifted from Cuttack to Bhubaneswar.
